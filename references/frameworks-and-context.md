# Psychological Frameworks and Context

Read this file only when deeper psychological, developmental, cultural, family, sexuality or gender analysis is useful to the current decision.

## Framework selection

Do not explain every case with attachment theory. Use the framework that best fits the evidence and distinguish empirical work from clinical or interpretive models.

- Bowlby, Ainsworth, Mikulincer and Shaver: attachment system, separation, anxiety, avoidance and regulation strategies
- Sue Johnson / EFT: attachment injuries and negative interaction cycles
- Gottman, Gottman and Levenson: criticism, contempt, defensiveness, stonewalling and repair
- Harriet Lerner: pursuer-distancer and overfunctioning-underfunctioning patterns
- Terrence Real: accountability, shame and relational repair
- Bowen and McGoldrick: differentiation, triangulation, family systems, culture and migration
- James Gross: emotion regulation, reappraisal and suppression
- Eisenberger, Baumeister and Leary: rejection, social pain and belonging; avoid sensational equivalence claims
- Kristin Neff: self-compassion and shame reduction
- Pennebaker: structured expressive writing, distinguished from rumination
- Pauline Boss: ambiguous loss
- William Worden: flexible grief tasks, not rigid stages
- Esther Perel: desire, autonomy, commitment and infidelity meaning-making; interpretive, not statistical law

Popular books may make concepts accessible but must not outrank primary research or case evidence. Never fabricate quotations, page numbers, effects or study findings.

## Developmental restraint

Record known caregiving, family stability, conflict, neglect, conditional approval, control, parentification, financial instability, separation, sibling role and autonomy support. A developmental hypothesis requires childhood facts plus repeated adult behaviour. Do not infer trauma from coldness or breakup alone.

## Culture and geography

Confirm actual location, cultural background, migration history, religion, class, family traditionalism, communication platforms, work/study and relocation plans. Interpret actions against the specific family or community baseline, not a national stereotype.

## Family and support systems

Assess warmth, stability, boundary respect, finances, partner acceptance, gender and marriage expectations, parenting interference, hierarchy and conflict handling. Separately assess whether the partner can make adult decisions when family and partner interests conflict.

The relationship subject may be a partner, parent, adult child, sibling, extended family member, in-law, friend or chosen family. Route the case before applying a romantic framework.

## LGBTQ+ and relationship contracts

Do not assume heterosexuality, cisgender identity, monogamy, public relationships or traditional family structures. Confirm the actual agreement.

Possible variables, never group assumptions:

- monogamy or openness, app boundaries, sexual-health agreements and sex/commitment separation
- outness, outing risk, family or employment safety and financial dependence
- chosen family and overlapping friend/ex/hookup networks
- identity affirmation, pronouns, privacy, dysphoria-sensitive intimacy, transition and fertility planning
- bi erasure or demands to prove orientation

Being closeted is not wrongdoing by itself. Sex outside a relationship is not automatically cheating in an open relationship; assess the actual agreement, disclosure and safety rules.

Use accurate, non-stigmatizing language for HIV/STI/PrEP/PEP/testing and verify current medical information.
